# Audience Content System

## First-Run Interview

Before drafting for a publication, ask:

- Publication name and publishing surface.
- Audience: who they are, what they know, what they do not know, and what they care about.
- Content formats with length and structure, such as short tip, concept explainer, tutorial, teardown, or briefing.
- Publishing cadence.
- 2-3 examples of pieces that landed well.

If any of these are missing, ask and stop before producing final content.

## Audience Contract

For each publication, maintain or infer only after confirmation:

- Knowledge floor: what the least technical reader already understands.
- Knowledge ceiling: where the piece can stretch them without losing them.
- Banned jargon and plain-language substitutions.
- Practical expectation: what readers can do after reading.
- Tone and voice constraints, optionally via `my-voice`.

## Format Templates

Create a template per format:

```markdown
## Format: Name
- Use when:
- Target length:
- Structure:
- Required sections:
- Optional sections:
- Ending/action:
- Example that landed:
```

Do not flatten all content into one generic structure.

## Batch Planning Mode

When Eric gives a theme, propose a full week or cycle before drafting:

```markdown
# Batch Plan: Theme

## Audience Goal

## Pieces
1. Format - Title - Reader job - Why this order

## Sequence Rationale

## Draft First
- Shortest/highest-leverage piece:
```

Only draft after the batch plan is accepted, unless Eric explicitly asks to draft immediately.

## Calibration Check

Before delivering any draft, ask internally:

`Would my least technical reader follow every step?`

If not, revise by adding missing context, replacing jargon, or narrowing scope. Do not dumb down the core idea; make the path clearer.

## Output Rules

- Use the configured format.
- Keep examples practical and audience-specific.
- Avoid unexplained jargon.
- Avoid content that assumes the audience already agrees with Eric's thesis.
- If a claim needs current facts, verify and source it.
